Turnaround supply readiness guide

A late purchase order and a purchase need are different problems.

A material can be ordered and still be unavailable when the job needs it. Turnaround review should separate quantity coverage from delivery timing.

Start with the need date

The meaningful question is not only how much supply exists. It is how much usable supply is available by the request need date. A single total that combines stock and every open purchase order can hide a timing gap.

Four positions to keep separate

  • Usable stock: inventory that can actually be allocated after blocked, quality-held, and reserved quantities are considered.
  • On-time open PO: ordered quantity expected within the accepted date basis before the material is needed.
  • Late open PO: ordered quantity that may cover the demand eventually but requires expediting, rescheduling, or another decision.
  • Additional purchase need: the remaining uncovered quantity after eligible stock and on-time supply are allocated.
Planning implication

If a request needs 8 units, usable stock covers 2, and an open PO for 6 arrives after the need date, the arithmetic total is 8. The need-date position is still exposed. The late PO is an expediting problem; it should not be reported as on-time coverage or automatically treated as a new purchase requirement.

Allocation across jobs

The same stock cannot cover every request independently. A review needs a stated priority and allocation order so earlier or higher-priority demand is considered before the next line. Otherwise the workbook can promise the same units more than once.

Know the model boundary

MRO Optix Individual v2.1 separates these positions for a bounded Excel review. The current edition supports one open PO quantity and due date per material and plant. Reviews that depend on split deliveries or multiple dated receipt lines need a method that represents that detail. The product has no live ERP connector and does not write procurement instructions.
