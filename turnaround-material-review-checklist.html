Turnaround material-review preparation checklist.

Use this before the review meeting to expose missing inputs, separate evidence from approval, and make need-date supply questions explicit.

For bounded material reviewsUse with fictional or authorised data onlyPrintable

Operating note: This checklist does not approve material requirements or replace site procedures, engineering judgement, procurement controls, or source-data verification. Do not place confidential plant data in an unapproved tool or channel.

Mark on paper or save an approved internal copy under your organisation’s document controls.

01

Define the review boundary

Agree what is being reviewed before assembling rows.

  • Turnaround/event, site, area, unit, and need-date basis are named.
  • The responsible planner, materials reviewer, engineering approver, and procurement contact are identified.
  • Included and excluded jobs/material classes are explicit.
  • The source-system cut-off date/time is recorded.
  • Request and reference volumes fit the chosen review method; nothing is silently truncated.

02

Confirm source ownership and versions

Know who owns each extract and whether it represents the intended snapshot.

  • Request list owner and revision are recorded.
  • Material master and plant-specific attributes come from an authorised source.
  • Consumption history includes a documented event-selection rule.
  • BOM extract scope and effective-date basis are understood.
  • Stock and open-PO extracts share a suitable cut-off.
  • Operational identifiers are handled only in approved locations.
03

Validate keys, units, and fields

Resolve exceptions before discussing a proposed quantity.

  • Material/plant keys match across request, master, history, BOM, stock, and PO data.
  • Duplicate request lines and duplicate reference keys are understood and resolved.
  • Units of measure align or an authorised conversion is documented.
  • Blank, unknown, inactive, obsolete, and superseded material statuses are reviewed.
  • Negative, zero, non-numeric, or unusually large quantities are explained.
  • Need dates and PO due dates are valid and use the same date basis.

04

Test the evidence for comparability

Historical use is context only when the comparison is defensible.

  • Comparable events reflect a similar asset, work scope, and operating context.
  • Abnormal events, one-off failures, and known data gaps are labelled.
  • Installed BOM presence is checked but not treated as automatic demand.
  • Mandatory scope and critical-spare rationale are recorded separately from historical use.
  • Event slots or history windows are sufficient for this review; any limit is visible.
  • A reviewer can explain why each evidence source is relevant to the decision.
05

Separate need-date supply positions

Coverage and timing are different questions.

  • Usable stock is distinguished from blocked, reserved, quality-held, or otherwise unavailable stock.
  • Stock allocation rules across competing jobs are understood.
  • On-time open-PO quantity is separated from late open-PO quantity.
  • Each PO date represents the intended receipt/date basis.
  • Split deliveries or multiple receipt lines are handled by a method that supports them.
  • Additional purchase need is reviewed after usable stock and eligible on-time supply.
  • Late supply is assigned for follow-up; it is not confused with uncovered demand.

06

Record the human decision

A proposed number must not silently become an approval.

  • Requested quantity and its source revision remain visible.
  • Any proposed change is labelled as a proposal, not a saving or approval.
  • The decision basis cites relevant evidence and known limitations.
  • Approved quantity is recorded separately and fixed to the decision record.
  • Decision owner, date, and supporting reference are complete.
  • A changed request, history, event set, rule, or material record triggers reconfirmation.
  • The responsible organisation verifies outputs before procurement or maintenance action.
07

Quality-check the review pack

Make the result reproducible enough for the next reviewer.

  • Filters cover the complete prepared range and no relevant rows are hidden unintentionally.
  • Formulas, validation rules, and intended input areas behave as expected.
  • Exceptions are resolved or explicitly carried forward with an owner.
  • Totals reconcile to the row-level review at the chosen cut-off.
  • The file saves, reopens, recalculates, filters, and prints correctly in the approved environment.
  • File name, version, checksum or other local release identifier, and retention location are recorded.
  • Handoff recipients understand what the workbook does not do.

Review handoff

Before action, state the remaining exceptions and confirm that proposed changes are not realised savings or procurement instructions.
