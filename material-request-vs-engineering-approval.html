Turnaround material review guide

A material request is not an engineering approval.

A request begins the review. It does not finish it. Keeping requested, proposed, and approved quantities separate gives planners and engineers a visible decision trail.

Three quantities, three meanings

  • Requested quantity records what the job or work pack asked for at a stated revision.
  • Proposed quantity is a review output based on the supplied evidence and rules. It is a question for the responsible reviewer.
  • Approved quantity records the human decision, together with the owner, date, and supporting reference.

When these fields are collapsed into one number, the organisation loses the difference between demand, analysis, and authority. A recommendation may appear to be an instruction. A revised request may silently invalidate an earlier approval.

Fictional example

A request contains 20 units. Comparable event history and the chosen review rule produce a proposal of 5 units. The correct next step is engineering review. Until an authorised reviewer confirms a quantity and the decision basis, 5 is neither an approved demand nor a realised saving.

Reconfirmation matters

A recorded approval depends on its evidence. If the request quantity, material record, comparable events, history, or rule set changes, the earlier decision basis has changed. The workflow should flag the line for reconfirmation rather than allow an old approval to follow new data.

A practical review record

For each decision, retain the request revision, proposed quantity, decision, approved quantity, owner, date, and evidence reference. Keep the confirmed basis fixed with the decision so a later reviewer can see whether the inputs have moved.

Where MRO Optix fits

MRO Optix Individual v2.1 is an Excel workflow designed around this separation. It supports a bounded review of up to 250 request lines, with up to 500 records in each master, material and plant history, and BOM table. It supports eight event slots and one open PO quantity and date per material and plant. It has no live ERP integration and does not automate engineering approval.
